WHO WE ARE

About the Chapter

Chartered in 2014, the ACM Student Chapter at Madhav Institute of Technology and Science has grown into the engineering hub for competitive coders, open-source maintainers, and computational researchers.

We bridge the critical divide between theoretical classroom curricula and high-velocity engineering practices. By providing direct access to cutting-edge technical tracks, algorithmic mentorship, and project incubators, we equip students to engineer fault-tolerant systems and scalable software architectures.

Through our annual hackathons, peer-led workshops, research symposiums, and code challenges, our chapter creates a collaborative sanctuary where visionary developers transform complex algorithms into real-world technological solutions.

Rules, Compilers & Eligibility

AlgoQuest is open to all enrolled undergraduate engineering and computer science students across India. Both on-campus students at MITS Gwalior (Lab 101 seats) and remote participants registered through CodeChef / HackerRank are eligible for all primary cash prizes and rankings.
Standard ACM-ICPC accepted languages: C++ (C++20 via g++ 13), Java (OpenJDK 21), Python (PyPy 3.10 and CPython 3.11), and Rust (1.75). Python is given a 2x time limit multiplier, but participants are strongly urged to use C++ for tight execution bounds in Division 1.
Rankings are determined primarily by the total number of problems accepted. Ties are resolved by lowest cumulative penalty time. Penalty time equals the submission minute of each accepted solution plus a 20-minute penalty for every rejected submission prior to acceptance.
All code undergoes automated cross-checking using Stanford's MOSS (Measure of Software Similarity) against all internal contest submissions, public GitHub repositories, and AI prompt databases. Any detected collaboration or generative AI code generation results in immediate disqualification and a permanent ban from ACM MITS Chapter activities.
